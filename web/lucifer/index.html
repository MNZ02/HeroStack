THE FIRST FALL · NINE DAYS DESCENDING

How bright the fall of the morning star.

Scroll, and begin the nine days.

I · GRACE

The morning
star

First-born of the dawn’s heralds, fairest of the seraphim. His light did not reflect the sun — the sun reflected him, and he knew it, and the knowing was the beginning.

II · GRACE

The first
thought

No thunder announced it. Sin arrived the way dusk does — a glance upward held one moment past worship, a question shaped like a crown.

III · GRACE

The decree

One archangel knelt too slowly, and Heaven noticed. The sentence needed no trumpet: a door opened beneath the brightest thing in creation.

IV · THE FALL

Cast down

There was no war worth the name, whatever the hymns claim. There was a threshold, and then there was gravity — and grace peeling off him like burning feathers.

V · THE FALL

Nine days

Nine days he fell, a red line struck through creation. Every world saw it at once: a second sunrise moving the wrong way, beautiful precisely because it was ending.

VI · THE FALL

White wings
turning to night

Gold drained from the skin; the six wings blackened from the tips inward, ember-light veining through the cracks like dawn in reverse. Each feather that left was a vow unmade.

VII · THE FALL

Chaos

Between heaven and hell there is no up, no down, no hour. Only the fall, remembering itself, and the wind of it shouting his name back at him — the last time anyone said it as an angel’s.

VIII · ABYSS

The burning
lake

He struck it like a sun refusing to set. The fire did not consume him — it recognized him, parted, and offered him a seat.

IX · ABYSS

Rise,
Lucifer.

The dark below was never a punishment. It is a kingdom — and it had been keeping a throne warm for exactly this kind of light.
